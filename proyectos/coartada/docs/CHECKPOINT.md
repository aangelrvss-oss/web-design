### Balance (bots, not people)

Perfect-logic bot innocents vs. three scripted culprit strategies; catch rate of the culprit:

| Culprit strategy | 4 players | 6 players | 8 players | 10 players | Mean (4–10) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lie only at the crime hour | 71% | 59% | 52% | 45% | 56% |
| Also lie at one other hour | 49% | 46% | 43% | 43% | 45% |
| Lie at every hour | 56% | 39% | 40% | 41% | 44% |

Read this as "the information structure is neither trivial nor hopeless and no single culprit strategy dominates". It is **not** a statement that roles are balanced for humans: the bots don't read faces, bluff socially or make mistakes, and real tables will differ. Two tuning decisions came from this data: (1) the culprit is no longer told where the accomplice was at the crime hour (with it, 6+ player catch rates fell to roughly 2–25% in an earlier version of the simulation); (2) small groups get fewer memories about the culprit and fewer clearable decoys.

## Critical QA: the five highest-impact problems found and fixed

These came from driving the real UI in browsers and reviewing the screenshots. This is my own assessment, not human playtest feedback.

1. **Lock-order tell (fairness).** Innocents' alibis were pre-filled with the truth, so they could seal instantly while the culprit had to think, and the public "who has sealed" list would point at the culprit. Now nobody gets pre-filled answers; everyone taps three places (the true ones are marked privately), so sealing time says little.
2. **Board grid broken** (cells lost their positioning context, so the evidence ring covered the whole board). Fixed the grid selectors; verified on 390 and 320 px.
3. **Sideways jiggle on the verdict** (the stamp's entry animation briefly widened the page, and a fixed button became unclickable). Horizontal overflow is now clipped at screen level without breaking the sticky header.
4. **Overcrowded investigation controls** (three wrapping buttons, a three-line status). Shorter labels ("Estoy listo" / "Esperad"), counter badge for testimonies, status line names what you're waiting for ("3 de 5 listos para la prueba 2").
5. **Single-phone privacy edges.** The last person's private turn ran straight into the shared board, and a tie could hand the runoff vote to the person still holding the phone. Added reveal gates ("Todas las coartadas, selladas → Destapar") and hand-offs keyed to the phase.

Also: the declaration time was raised (Normal 90 → 120 s) because the first screen asks people to read the case and their file; the folder tab of the opened private file was being clipped by its reveal animation.

## Not done / limitations

- **No human playtest.** Fun, clarity in under 60 seconds and balance with real people are design hypotheses until a group plays several rounds.
- **No physical-device testing.** All browser testing used Chromium with emulated phone viewports and touch. Safari/iOS and Firefox were not exercised. Things to check by hand on real phones: safe-area insets, the keyboard over the name field, QR scanning, background-tab reconnection on iOS, audio unlock on iOS.
- **Not deployed.** No hosting credentials were available and the Docker daemon is not running in this environment, so the Docker image was not built here (the bundle it copies was verified standalone).
- **In-memory rooms.** A server restart ends all rooms. Run one instance only.
- **Generated art.** None, for the network reason above. The visual identity is CSS/SVG.
- **Encargos** are implemented and tested for correctness but not balance-simulated; off by default.
- **Large groups (9–10)** work but the board gets crowded.
- **Language.** Spanish (Spain) only; copy is centralized in `src/client/lib/text.ts` and the case files.
- Room-code guessing gives at most a lobby seat or a spectator view of public data; there is no per-IP join throttle beyond per-socket rate limits.
